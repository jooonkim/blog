## Why this matters

AI agents can now read your email, query live data, and draft customer deliverables from end to end. Speed is the most obvious use of these capabilities in customer-facing work. Producing the same meeting recap in two minutes instead of twenty saves time, but we must ask ourselves what else becomes possible.

[Felix Rieseberg](https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/) describes three other dimensions of ambition: altitude, range, and depth. In customer work, altitude means looking across the customer's business to understand the outcome they actually need. Range means drawing on disciplines such as data analysis, computer science, and domain expertise that we might previously have needed another team to provide. Depth means taking on questions and tasks that can transform a customer's business that we previously lacked the instruments or time to answer.

That ambition brings complexity. The agent has to combine information from several sources, choose appropriate methods, and explain recommendations that a customer may act on. We need a framework for deciding what it should know, how it should work, and what we need to verify.

My view comes from leading AI initiatives at Google, building agent workflows with clients and sales teams, and giving a guest lecture at UC Berkeley Haas Executive Education. I use **Know, Execute, Prove** across customer-facing roles, including enterprise sales, account management, solutions engineering, and advisory work:

- **Know:** Keep a small, dated Markdown file of what we know about each customer (`customers/<account>.md`).
- **Execute:** Give the agent written, reusable procedures in Markdown with YAML frontmatter (`skills/<workflow>/SKILL.md`) for recurring work.
- **Prove:** Check consequential claims against current, inspectable evidence from deterministic queries or primary records before a customer sees them.

The three parts depend on one another. A procedure needs customer context; a diagnosis needs live evidence; verified findings should update the customer record.

## Give the agent less to read, and better material

Models can accept long inputs, but a long context window does not guarantee that a buried fact will shape the answer. Stale information is especially troublesome. A May revenue target beside a September target can look like two equally valid instructions. An old report can sound current when its date is missing.

The practical response is to load information as the task requires it. This approach is often called **progressive disclosure**. The research on ["Lost in the Middle"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172), Chroma's work on ["Context Rot"](https://research.trychroma.com/context-rot), and Anthropic's guide to ["Effective Context Engineering for AI Agents"](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) help explain why it works. I treat context as an attention budget to curate: include current material that helps answer the question and leave the rest on disk for retrieval.

One practical way to manage progressive disclosure is with plain Markdown (`.md`) files. Where your agent supports it, short YAML frontmatter (`---`) can help it find the right file. Instead of pasting every rule, account history, and playbook into a single prompt, split the agent's workspace into four distinct layers:

Adding more context often feels like diligence. If we give the agent every document and explain every exception, we hope it will make fewer mistakes. The difficulty is that we also give it more opportunities to use an outdated target, follow a superseded instruction, or mistake an example for a fact.

### What to load for the next task

Suppose you are preparing a renewal review. A familiar prompt might be: *"Read these twelve documents, revisit the conversation where we made last quarter's deck, and follow that workflow with all the goals we discussed."* Before running it, decide what the agent needs now:

- Load the current customer Markdown file (`customers/<account>.md`), the renewal question, and the skill for preparing the review (`skills/renewal-review/SKILL.md`).
- Keep the contract, usage reports, and previous deck available on disk or via tools to open when a specific step needs them.
- Move superseded targets and abandoned draft instructions out of the active context. Preserve their sources as dated links in an archive or ledger if you need to explain what changed.

The resulting prompt can be: *"Prepare a renewal review using `customers/acme.md` and `skills/renewal-review/SKILL.md`. Confirm the renewal date in the contract and pull usage for the agreed reporting period. Use last quarter's deck as a layout reference. Flag conflicting goals before making a recommendation."*

After a successful task, save the procedure and final deliverable where the agent can retrieve them. Reconstructing the workflow from a long conversation forces the next run to sift through failed attempts as well.

### Decide what to trust

I rank inputs in the following order when preparing customer advice, and treat anything below the top two as something to verify before citing:

1. **Live Deterministic Query.**
   - **Source:** SQL query, reporting API call, or primary system record run in the current session
   - **Use:** Cite directly; save the query, parameters, and timestamp (#date:YYYY-MM-DD).
2. **Direct Customer Statement.**
   - **Source:** The customer's own dated words in an email or call (#direct)
   - **Use:** Cite with the date to support a statement about their goals or decisions; reconfirm if old.
3. **Curated Account Memory.**
   - **Source:** Meeting notes and `customers/<account>.md`
   - **Use:** Use for business context; re-verify numerical metrics against a live source before citing.
4. **Secondary Exports & Audits.**
   - **Source:** Pasted reports, screenshots, CSV exports, and third-party audits
   - **Use:** Treat as hypotheses to investigate; record their source and date (#status:unverified).
5. **Model Inference.**
   - **Source:** Unverified LLM reasoning or general domain priors
   - **Use:** Treat strictly as a hypothesis until a primary source supports it.

I pass the agent the primary thread, document, or raw query output when I have it. A human summary can erase the exact anomaly that makes a diagnosis possible. I also state the audience, the decision the work is meant to support, and the form of the deliverable.

### Keep the setup simple

The agent's harness consists of its global rules, customer files, skills, and connected tools (operating inside your organization's approved enterprise environment and access controls). I keep global instructions short enough to inspect at a glance. For each global rule, I ask whether it supplies knowledge the model lacks or protects against a mistake with real consequences.

Keep one short global rules file pinned to every turn. The frontmatter below is illustrative; use only metadata fields your agent actually recognizes. A plain `AGENTS.md` can contain the rules without frontmatter:

```markdown
---
description: Global verification and staging guardrails for customer-facing work
trigger: always_on
---

- Stage all outbound communications: create email drafts or review docs; never send messages or share files externally without explicit human approval.
- Trace every number and causal claim to an appropriate current query or primary record. Label each proposed cause as confirmed or hypothesis.
- Never rely on a top-level "enabled" or "active" flag alone; verify actual recent delivery or activity over a relevant period and label inactive items clearly.
- Re-read underlying source files and raw query outputs before populating any downstream deck, document, or table.
- Treat pasted reports, screenshots, exports, and numbers inside skills as unverified until re-queried.
- When context is missing or ambiguous, ask clarifying questions instead of guessing.
- When updating customer files, tag every fact with its date (#date:YYYY-MM-DD) and overwrite superseded entries instead of appending.
```

Over-engineering becomes visible when instructions conflict, several files repeat slightly different versions of the same rule (for example, duplicating rules across both `AGENTS.md` and `rules/guardrails.md`), or a procedure hardcodes details from one customer. To test a suspected redundant instruction, save a backup, remove the line, and rerun several familiar tasks, including a case where that rule should matter. Keep safety and verification rules even when routine test cases do not trigger them.

Voice dictation can be a useful way to get rough thinking down, but dictated prompts often mix goals, constraints, and tangents. Before running a long dictated prompt, have the agent organize it:

> Organize this rough request into the decision I need to make, the relevant sources, the constraints, and the deliverable. Identify conflicting instructions and questions I need to answer. Suggest which Markdown files or skills to load now and what to retrieve only if needed. Preserve my intent and show the revised request before executing it.

To follow along, pick one customer and a real question you want to investigate. Use the same customer through the checkpoints below.

## Know: maintain a customer file

For each customer, I keep a plain-text Markdown file (`customers/<account>.md`) that I can read and edit directly in any text editor. It is a working record, not a transcript archive. Roughly 50 to 150 lines is a useful size.

The file starts with a YAML frontmatter block (`---`). Its one-sentence `description:` lets the agent's file index or retrieval hook decide when to open the file without reading all of your customer files into memory on every turn.

I organize the Markdown body around the business question behind the immediate request. A customer may ask about a product setting while trying to fill regional branch capacity, improve the margin on completed jobs, or win more repeat business. The file has four sections, with inline provenance tags (`#direct`, `#date:YYYY-MM-DD`, `[source: ...]`) on every material fact. The following account is fictional; its figures, records, and dates are illustrative:

```markdown
---
description: Multi-region B2B software account focused on Q4 seat expansion and reducing onboarding drop-off across 12 regional branches.
last_updated: 2026-10-01
---

## 1. The customer's customers
- End buyer: Mid-market operations teams purchasing workflow automation to reduce manual ticket routing.
- Channel and branch dynamics: 12 regional branches sell and implement locally; corporate HQ controls pricing tiers and renewal terms.
- Margin driver: Annual enterprise subscriptions (78% gross margin) and multi-year implementation renewals.

## 2. Stakeholders and unit economics
- VP of Operations (Executive Sponsor): Prioritizes branch adoption rate and time-to-first-workflow [source: Q3 QBR call, #direct, #date:2026-09-14].
- RevOps Lead (Technical Owner): Owns CRM-to-product telemetry sync and seat provisioning.
- Unit economics: $18,000 average annual contract value; target payback under 9 months; renewal threshold requires >=70% active seat utilization.

## 3. Verified footprint
- Active deployment: 420 provisioned seats across 12 branches; 310 monthly active seats as of #date:2026-09-28 [source: queries/active_seats_by_branch.sql].
- Inactive legacy workspaces: 2 regional sandbox workspaces remain marked ENABLED in billing but have 0 active users over the last 14 days (PAUSED).
- Data pipeline: Daily webhook sync from customer CRM to product analytics; known 48-hour attribution lag on offline contract upgrades.

## 4. Dated strategy ledger
- 2026-09-14 (#direct): Agreed to pilot automated onboarding playbooks in 3 branches before expanding contract-wide [source: Q4 Pilot Proposal Doc].
- Open question (#status:unconfirmed): Why did Midwest branch active seats fall 25% in September? Pending cohort export from RevOps Lead.
```

When a decision changes, I overwrite the old line and retain a link to the earlier source if history matters. That gives the agent one current version to use. I explicitly tag unresolved questions with `#status:unconfirmed` so they cannot quietly become facts in a later draft.

### Checkpoint 1: make the customer file retrievable

Give your agent a recent customer email thread and the notes or records you trust. Use this prompt:

> Draft a customer Markdown file (`customers/<account>.md`) using these sources. Start with a YAML frontmatter block containing a one-sentence `description:` and `last_updated: YYYY-MM-DD`. Organize the Markdown body into four H2 sections: `1. The customer's customers`, `2. Stakeholders and unit economics`, `3. Verified footprint`, and `4. Dated strategy ledger`. Attach a `[source: ...]` and `#date:YYYY-MM-DD` tag (plus `#direct` for direct customer statements) to each material fact. Mark missing information as `#status:unknown` and surface conflicting facts for my review. Show me the Markdown file before saving it.

Correct the file, then save it in your workspace's `customers/` folder and add its path and one-sentence description to your index if your tool does not index YAML frontmatter automatically. Start a fresh chat and ask the agent for the customer's current goal and most recent agreed decision, with sources. You should be able to open those sources and confirm both answers.

### What to carry into a fresh chat

Start a new chat when the task changes or the conversation has accumulated enough dead ends that it is hard to follow. Copying an entire transcript into a new session also copies its stale assumptions. Instead, have the agent write a compact `handoff.md` file (or paste a short YAML/Markdown handoff block) and let the new session open the canonical customer file and skill fresh:

```markdown
---
task: Q4 renewal and Midwest branch usage diagnosis
customer_file: customers/acme.md
skill: skills/usage-drop-diagnosis/SKILL.md
date: 2026-10-02
---

## Verified findings
- Provisioned seats: 420 across 12 branches; active seats fell from 120 to 90 (-25.0%) in the Midwest region between August and September [source: queries/midwest_active_users.sql, #date:2026-10-02].
- Billing and contract entitlements are unchanged [source: contract_ledger_v4, #date:2026-10-02].

## Open questions (#status:unconfirmed)
- Did the September 10 SSO identity-provider migration break automated seat provisioning in the 3 Midwest branches?

## Next step
- Run `scripts/check_sso_provisioning_logs.sql` for the 3 Midwest branch IDs before drafting the customer reply.
```

## Execute: write procedures for work you repeat

A **skill** is a written procedure stored as a `SKILL.md` file inside its own folder (`skills/<workflow-name>/SKILL.md`), optionally paired with deterministic query templates or helper scripts (`skills/<workflow-name>/scripts/`) and reference templates (`skills/<workflow-name>/references/`).

In agents that support skill metadata, the YAML frontmatter at the top of `SKILL.md` helps the agent decide *when* to load the full file. A sharp `description:` states both what the skill does and the exact trigger conditions ("Use when... Don't use for...").

The first four skills I recommend building for a customer-facing team cover four recurring jobs:

/>

These `SKILL.md` files should specify *how* to find a number (the exact SQL query, API endpoint, or script to run), and use `<PLACEHOLDERS>` for account IDs, dates, and thresholds. They should **never** contain a real customer's live number as an inline example. A later draft may cite that sample number as a fact.

Here is a concrete, minimal `skills/usage-drop-diagnosis/SKILL.md` template:

```markdown
---
name: usage-drop-diagnosis
description: Diagnoses a change or drop in customer product usage, conversion volume, or pipeline health. Use when a customer or teammate asks why a metric changed. Don't use for initial account footprint discovery or renewal pricing proposals.
---

## 1. Context to load first
- Open `customers/<account>.md` and read the verified footprint, data pipeline notes, and dated strategy ledger.
- Read the customer's incoming email thread or ticket verbatim.

## 2. Hypothesis generation and deterministic verification
- List plausible causes before proposing an explanation (e.g., configuration override, data-sync or webhook failure, permission/SSO change, seasonal schedule, or upstream demand shift).
- Run `scripts/compare_period_metrics.sql` for `<ACCOUNT_ID>` across `<CURRENT_PERIOD>` and `<PRIOR_PERIOD>`.
- Verify which workspaces or units are actively serving (`serving_status = 'ACTIVE'` with delivery in the last 14 days) versus `(PAUSED)` or `(ENDED)`.
- Compute all percentages, ratios, and deltas inside the SQL query or Python script rather than in the drafted prose.
- Sweep all other active workspaces or branches in `<ACCOUNT_ID>` to check whether the pattern is isolated or account-wide.

## 3. Methodology checklist (apply, don't recite)
- Check stakeholders in `customers/<account>.md` so the tone and level of detail match the reader.
- Connect the recommendation to the customer's stated business goal and unit economics.
- Trace every number and causal statement to a current query or primary source you can inspect.
- Separate confirmed findings from open questions requiring customer or partner input.

## 4. Deliverable and human-in-the-loop gate
- Output a 3-column verification table (`Proposed claim`, `Query / evidence source`, `Review status`) before the draft.
- Stage a concise reply draft (3–4 plain-English bullets) and place detailed tables and step-by-step fixes in a companion document.
- Propose dated `#date:YYYY-MM-DD` updates to `customers/<account>.md` for review. Never send emails or share documents externally.
```

Notice Section 3 of that skill. Most organizations have a sales or advisory methodology they want reflected in customer work. Pasting a 60-slide methodology deck into your global rules wastes attention on every turn. Compressing it into four or five checkable lines inside the drafting skill keeps the output aligned with how your team coaches without bloating the context window.

### Checkpoint 2: save one procedure you can reuse

Choose a workflow connected to your customer question, such as preparing a renewal review or diagnosing a change in product usage. Ask your agent:

> Draft a reusable `SKILL.md` file for this workflow. Start with a YAML frontmatter block containing `name:` (kebab-case) and a specific `description:` stating when to load the skill and what it is not for. In the Markdown body, specify: (1) which `customers/<account>.md` sections and primary sources to open first, (2) what deterministic checks or queries to run before making a recommendation, (3) how to verify calculations and active statuses, and (4) what staged deliverable to prepare for human review. Use `<PLACEHOLDERS>` for all customer-specific values. Show me the complete `SKILL.md` file.

Review the steps against how you do the work yourself. Save the file under `skills/<workflow-name>/SKILL.md`. In a fresh chat, give the agent a task that should trigger the skill, and ask it to report which `SKILL.md` it loaded and which checks it completed or could not complete. Revise any step that was skipped or ambiguous.

## Prove: investigate before drafting an explanation

When a customer asks why a result changed, the fastest answer from a language model is a fluent guess. A drop in adoption or output might reflect customer demand, a product configuration change, a broken integration, a reporting gap, or a staffing constraint on the customer's team. Asking a language model *"are you sure?"* lets the model grade its own homework.

Prove pairs model reasoning with deterministic tools (SQL queries, API calls, Python scripts, and primary logs) and gives each side the job it is built for:

1. **Ground.**
   - **Tool:** Reads `customers/<account>.md` and the verbatim email/ticket text.
   - **Agent:** Identifies the business decision at stake and relevant stakeholders.
2. **Hypothesize.**
   - **Agent:** Lists candidate causes across product config, data pipeline, permissions, and demand.
3. **Validate.**
   - **Tool:** Runs queries for configuration history, active vs. paused status, event counts by action, and integration error logs.
   - **Agent:** Selects which queries to run next and eliminates unsupported causes.
4. **Compute.**
   - **Tool:** Calculates all deltas, percentages, and ratios in SQL or Python.
   - **Agent:** The model interprets the calculated result; it does not invent the figure.
5. **Widen.**
   - **Tool:** Loops the verification query across all active branches, workspaces, or campaigns.
   - **Agent:** Prioritizes which affected units need immediate remediation vs. monitoring.
6. **Synthesize.**
   - **Tool:** Copies verified figures directly from query outputs and logs the exact queries in an appendix.
   - **Agent:** Writes clear, partner-friendly prose and targeted questions for technical owners.
7. **Update Memory.**
   - **Tool:** Writes verified metrics with #date:YYYY-MM-DD and [source: ...] tags into `customers/<account>.md`.
   - **Agent:** Identifies which findings are durable enough to replace stale entries.

One quick reliability test is to run the diagnosis a second time in a fresh chat while holding the source data, reporting period, and metric definitions constant. The prose wording will vary, but every reported number, status, and calculation should match. If a number changes between runs, inspect the query, filters, source version, and calculation. Matching numbers still need review because both runs can repeat the same mistake.

### Checks to keep in a diagnostic procedure

Regardless of industry or product, seven checks help catch common diagnostic failures in customer-facing agent workflows:

- **Separate active records from legacy or paused shells:** Never trust a top-level `status = ENABLED` flag alone. Check actual delivery or usage over a relevant recent period and explicitly label dormant items as `(PAUSED)` or `(ENDED)` so you never recommend optimizing an inactive setup.
- **Distinguish optimization signals from total logged events:** Many systems record dozens of event types while using only a subset to drive automated workflows, routing, or billing. Verify which events are configured as primary triggers.
- **Check local overrides before trusting account-wide defaults:** Workspace-, branch-, or campaign-level settings frequently override parent account defaults without surfacing a warning.
- **Compare single-unit issues against account-wide patterns:** Before blaming a local configuration edit, check whether the same drop occurred across all workspaces or branches. A shared drop may suggest a wider data or integration change.
- **Inspect integration payloads and zero-value records:** External CRM and partner pipeline changes rarely appear in product audit logs. Check upload diagnostics, error rates, and whether events are arriving with `0` or `null` values before launching value-based automation.
- **Account for ingestion and attribution lag:** When evaluating changes made within the last few days, check the system's reporting latency and whether events are attributed by interaction date or completion date.
- **Re-read raw source outputs before generating downstream deliverables:** Never populate a customer deck, document, or spreadsheet from a compacted chat summary alone; open the saved query output (open the saved source file) and verify every row 1:1.

### Checkpoint 3: inspect the evidence before the reply

Run your customer question using the `customers/<account>.md` file and `SKILL.md` procedure you saved. Before accepting a draft, ask:

> Show a verification table of every material claim you plan to include in the reply, its exact query or primary source, its `#date:YYYY-MM-DD` tag, and whether it is `confirmed`, `provisional`, or `unsupported`. For numerical claims, show the query or script calculation. For each proposed cause, show the supporting evidence and the alternative explanations you tested and ruled out. Then stage a concise reply draft whose wording reflects those limits, and propose exact line-item updates to `customers/<account>.md`. Leave sending and external sharing for my review.

Open the source behind the main recommendation, check one calculation, and inspect one ruled-out alternative. Correct the draft, approve the verified updates to `customers/<account>.md`, and run a second question through the same workflow to confirm the setup is reusable.

## Match review to the cost of being wrong

Not every task needs the same ceremony. Calibrate verification depth to the cost of a wrong answer:

1. **Private Exploration.**
   - **Examples:** Brainstorming hypotheses, learning a new industry, organizing rough notes
   - **Review:** Model inference is fine; label outputs #status:unverified and do not save to `customers/<account>.md`.
2. **Internal Planning.**
   - **Examples:** Account plans, internal prep notes, team readouts
   - **Review:** Every metric traces to a query with #date:YYYY-MM-DD; causes are labeled confirmed or hypothesis.
3. **Customer-Facing Deliverables.**
   - **Examples:** Client emails, strategy docs, QBR decks, diagnostic readouts
   - **Review:** Full Prove loop, verification table, open the saved source file check against raw query output, human review, and staged drafts only.
4. **High-Stakes Commitments.**
   - **Examples:** Pricing/budget changes, root-cause escalations, forecasts, or contractual terms
   - **Review:** Tier 3 plus a second human reviewer, conservative phrasing, and projections tied to an approved forecast source.

The cost of a plausible but wrong answer in customer work is concrete: a customer may reallocate budget, alter a rollout, or change a technical integration on the strength of your advice. My rule of thumb is simple: only include claims you would feel comfortable explaining to the customer's CFO with the underlying query and source record open on screen.

## Clean up a setup you already use

Some apparent hallucinations begin in the agent's own workspace. The model may repeat stale or hardcoded material with confidence. I call this **context poisoning**:

- A `SKILL.md` containing a hardcoded sample figure (for example, `"target payback is 6 months"` or `"baseline usage is 450 seats"`) that the agent later cites as a real customer's metric.
- A pasted export or screenshot summary sitting in `customers/<account>.md` for months without a `#date:YYYY-MM-DD` tag.
- Duplicate or slightly different versions of global rules scattered across `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, and `rules/*.md`.

Before changing an established setup, save a backup copy of your working files. Then ask the agent to inventory what it can actually inspect.

### Checkpoint 4: audit for context poisoning before archiving

Run this maintenance prompt monthly, or whenever your agent starts producing oddly specific unqueried numbers:

> Inventory the global rule files (`AGENTS.md`, `rules/*.md`), skill files (`skills/*/SKILL.md`), and customer files (`customers/*.md`) you can inspect in this workspace, and list what you cannot inspect. Audit them for context poisoning:
> 1. Flag any hardcoded number, customer name, account ID, date, or pasted report inside a `SKILL.md`, and propose replacing it with a `<PLACEHOLDER>` or a live query script.
> 2. Flag any metric in `customers/*.md` that is past its useful review window or missing a `#date:YYYY-MM-DD` and `[source: ...]` tag, and propose marking it `#status:stale`.
> 3. Flag duplicate or conflicting rules across global instruction files and propose consolidating them into a single pinned file.
> Show me a table of proposed edits and their expected effect before modifying any file.

Archive superseded content outside the folders loaded by default, retain links where historical context matters, and rerun two or three familiar customer tasks on the same source data to confirm nothing regressed.

When a team adopts this workflow together, the shortest prompt is the least interesting part. The durable advantage comes from readable Markdown context (`Know`), modular `SKILL.md` procedures (`Execute`), and a verification loop that pairs model judgment with deterministic evidence before a customer ever sees the draft (`Prove`).
